# Form

Modelled on "Register a repository" (`/repositories`). The app posts forms to server actions
and re-renders the page; the stories show each submit state through props.

## Structure

`Card` > `CardHeader` (what the form does, in the title and description) > `CardContent` >
general error, the form row (labelled fields, then the one `default` button), the field error,
the status message.

## Rules

1. The label is always visible; the placeholder is an example. Source: WCAG 3.3.2, Input page.
2. A field error sits under its field, is linked with `aria-describedby`, uses
   `aria-invalid` and `role="alert"`. Source: WCAG 3.3.1, `app/repositories/page.tsx`.
3. After a submit with errors, focus moves to the first invalid field. Source: SPEC-122 prompt
   (stage 3); WCAG 3.3.1. The app does not do this yet: on "What is missing".
4. Entered data is kept after an error (`defaultValue` from the server's answer). Source:
   `app/repositories/page.tsx`.
5. While the submit runs, the field and the button are disabled and a `role="status"` line says
   what is happening; a second press does nothing. Source: SPEC-122 §18 P11.
6. An error that is not about one field goes above the form, says what happened and what to do.
   Source: SPEC-122 §18 Q1.
7. Success is a `role="status"` line that names the result and the next step; no toast.
8. One `default` button per form. Source: SPEC-122 §18 P12.
9. A card holds one form with a `default` button. Two independent forms (tags and notes) are
   two cards, not one card with two main actions. Source: SPEC-122 §18 P12, Card page.

## Lists edited in place

Modelled on Tags and Assigned benchmarks on `/artifacts/[id]` (issue 64).

1. Each item shows its value (a `secondary` Badge for a user label, a link for an item with a
   page) and its own small form with an `outline` `sm` button: "Remove", "Unassign". The
   button's accessible name names the item ("Remove tag security"). The Badge itself is never
   the button. Source: Badge page, Content page.
2. The field and `default` button that add an item sit under the list, in the same card.
3. The item forms and the add form share one result line and one pending state: one
   `useActionState` per card, so a remove and an add never show two messages at once.
4. Removing or unassigning a reversible item needs no confirmation, when its history stays
   (runs, results). Say so in the result line ("Its results stay in Results by version.").
   Source: Risky-action page.
5. After a remove, focus moves to the add field (Usage rule 5).
6. An item that is already there, or already gone, is a status, not an error ("security is
   already a tag.").
7. At the list's limit the add field and button are disabled and a sentence next to them says
   why and what to do, linked with `aria-describedby`.
8. An empty list is a sentence in the card ("No tags yet."), not an `EmptyState`.
